THE ORIGIN PROBLEM

A clean plot can be the exit door for a deforested one

Paper accepts anything. A deforestation-free plot has its permits in order and sells without trouble. But if it declares more tons than its land can produce, that difference comes from somewhere else, often from a plot that could never sell on its own.

That is how origin gets laundered: the grain or cocoa from a deforested plot travels with the papers of a clean one. Checking that the clean plot is clean is not enough. You have to control how much it sells.

WHAT WE OBSERVE FROM A SINGLE COORDINATE

Four sources watch the same plot, each asking something different

Public, recognized sources, the same ones used by governments and the European Union. Nothing proprietary you have to take on faith.

SENTINEL-2 · ESA / COPERNICUS
Was there a crop, and how strongly did it grow?
It measures vegetation vigor pass after pass and draws the season curve: sowing, growth and harvest.
SENTINEL-1 · RADAR
What happened while it was cloudy?

HOW THE DOOR GETS CLOSED

Three simple rules, none of them depends on a person

1
The land sets the ceiling
The plot's hectares times the reference yield of its crop: that is the most it can produce in a year. A deforested plot has its capacity withheld.
2
Every sale deducts
Every certified shipment deducts its tons from the balance of its origin plots. When the balance reaches zero, that plot can no longer back any sale that year.
3
The satellite checks
It measures how the plot really did: the season's vigor compared with its own previous years and with the plots around it. And it issues a risk signal.
GREEN
No deforestation, balance available and a crop cycle consistent with what was declared.
YELLOW
Something to look at: balance nearly used up, very fast sales, yield below its area, or nearby deforestation.
RED
Deforestation on the plot or right next to it, no balance left, or what was declared does not match what is seen from space.

The signal informs, it does not decide. It shows what is there so each buyer, bank or auditor can make their own decision. And it is sealed together with every shipment.

FEET ON THE GROUND

What the satellite measures, and what it doesn't

To trust a proof you have to know where it ends. This is what we claim and what we don't.

TONS
The satellite does not count tons. It measures whether there was a crop and how strongly it grew. The production ceiling comes from the hectares times the reference yield of each crop.
MEASURED YIELD
It is comparative: how that plot did against its own previous years and against the plots in its area. It is meant to detect what doesn't add up, not to weigh a harvest.
PERENNIAL CROPS
In cocoa, coffee or fruit trees the canopy stays green regardless of the harvest, so satellite yield does not apply. There, the control is the production ceiling, the balance and forest monitoring.
WHO DECIDES
EPIMELEIA is neither an auditor nor a regulator. It is a neutral record: it leaves the proof, and each party uses it before whoever it needs to.
